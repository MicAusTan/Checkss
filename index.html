Controls
Move: click a piece, then a square. Or drag the piece there (Esc puts it back).
Premove: while it is not your turn, drag one of your pieces to a square (clicking does nothing, so no accidents). You can queue several; the pieces show where they will be, so you can move them again. The first is played when your turn comes if it is still legal, otherwise the queue is cancelled and the chat says so. Esc or a click on the board cancels them. Not available in hidden-move modes.
Arrows and circles: right-drag draws an arrow, right-click a square draws a circle. Shift = red, Alt = yellow. Only you see them; a click on the board, Esc or the next move clears them.
Suggest a move to your team: hold Ctrl and right-drag from a piece to the square it should go to. You see the arrow as you drag. Dashed arrows are suggestions, you can make several, and bots take the advice for their teammates. Pointing at an opponent's piece shows what you expect it to do (dotted arrow, never advice). Only your team sees them. Ctrl + right-click a square removes the suggestions touching it, or all of yours if none does.
Big boards: mouse wheel zooms, dragging empty space pans, Fit all shows everything.
Turning things off: the gear at the top right has switches for sliding pieces, dragging, premoves and sounds.
This mode has no special rules to explain. It plays like the usual chess, with the changes named in its title. Start a game to see its guide here.

8-Player Chess rules & new pieces

Board

  • 24×24 cross: a 18×18 centre with a 3-deep arm on every side. Each arm holds two armies side by side, separated by a 2-square gap with a notch cut 2 deep, so neighbours can't slide along each other's back ranks. 524 squares.
  • Turn order goes around the board: Red → Orange → Blue → Pink → Yellow → Teal → Green → Purple. With fewer than 8 players the seats are spread evenly and empty seats have no army.
  • Teams: 2v2v2v2 (you and the army opposite you) or 4v4 (seats alternate around the board). A team is out when all its kings are.

Promotion & holes

  • Pawns promote 10 ranks from their start (halfway across the board).
  • Holes are squares that don't exist: nothing can enter them, sliders stop in front of them, knights jump over them. A pawn with a hole directly in front of it promotes where it stands. Select the pawn and click the hole.
  • Hole layouts: none, four small, one big, or random (mirrored for fairness, or truly random). Random layouts are revealed when the game starts and never wall anybody in.

Neutral pieces & the Boss

  • Neutrals (dashed ring) move by themselves once after every full round. Nothing here gives check. Only the first owner counts: a piece that has been owned and is captured again is gone for good.
  • With neutral drops on, capturing a wild Boar, Hound, Burrower or Golem puts it in your hand and you can drop it as your own piece.
  • Boss: a 2×2 monster that starts in the middle. It rolls over what it moves onto, crushing everything but kings, and gets faster as it loses cells. Capturing one of its cells also costs the piece that captured it. Taking the last cell lets you kill it (with drops off, a captured piece of yours comes back) or claim it: it re-enters in the middle as your own 2×2 piece, crushing everything there, kings included. An owned Boss moves up to 2 squares in straight lines, and with 3 or 4 cells it must rest a turn after moving.

Imperial Chess rules

Goal

  • Destroy the enemy army. The Imperials (Emperor Ⓔ, Empress Ⓧ, Heir Ⓗ) are special: with 2 or more alive there is no check. When exactly one is left it is the check target (normal check/checkmate). Imperials can check each other: if your move puts your Imperial and a lone enemy Imperial in each other's reach, you are the checker and are safe from that Imperial. It may not capture yours and must get out of danger; any other piece covering the square still makes the move illegal.
  • You lose immediately if only Imperials remain (no other pieces). Stalemate is a draw. Losing the King or Queen is never a loss.

Board

  • 17×16. The corner squares of each army's 3 ranks are cut away: nothing lands there or slides through. Pawns promote on the first full-width rank in front of the enemy (and deeper). Promotion is compulsory. Select an original Rook, Bishop or Knight and the zone where it would be promoted is tinted gold. One that reaches it is promoted automatically into its Grand version (Grand Rook / Bishop / Knight, which glow gold on the board and in the chat); pawns get a choice of pieces.

Imperials

  • Immune to pawn-family captures (Pawn, Pikeman, Skirmisher). Emperor also immune to Rooks, Empress to Bishops, Heir to Knights (Grand pieces ignore this).
  • Emperor: rook lines plus the squares beside the line. Empress: diagonals plus the 4 orthogonal neighbours of each line square. A piece on the line cuts off the side squares at and beyond it. Lose your Empress → the Emperor reaches only 3 squares and loses Rook immunity (and vice-versa). Heir: 16 knight + camel leaps; when both Emperor and Empress are gone he also gets (±2,±4),(±4,±2) and loses Knight immunity.
  • Castle: Emperor hops a friendly Rook/Grand Rook, Empress a friendly Bishop/Grand Bishop, landing on an empty square beyond it (max 6 away, cooldown 3 of your turns, not while in check). Swap: the Heir may swap with a friendly Knight/Grand Knight in his reach.

King & Queen auras

  • King (aura 7×7): friendly pawn-family walk one step further (2 on the 2-player and wide boards, 3 on the 4-player cross board); a pawn that leaves the aura by its own move keeps that bonus for its next turn only; Rooks, Bishops, Knights and Grands can't be captured by pawn-family pieces. The King can also move or capture onto any square beside a friendly piece within 2 squares of him (a bigger Guard hop).
  • Queen (aura 5×5): ordinary Pawns also capture straight ahead; Archers, Guards, Chariots, Sappers, Catapults can't be captured by pawn-family pieces.

Royals

  • Prince / Princess (promotion only, one each): Prince captures on the 5×5 (no corners) and also steps to corners and 3 straight; blockable. Princess moves 1, shoots the outer ring and 3 straight without moving; she can't be captured while the Prince is next to her.

Pieces

  • Grand Rook ♖ rook + knight moves (move only). Grand Bishop ♗ bishop + 1–2 straight. Grand Knight ♘ the whole outer ring of the 5×5. Only original Rooks/Bishops/Knights become Grands (automatically on reaching the promotion rank).
  • Guard Ⓖ king moves, plus steps 2 straight or onto squares around a nearby friend; Archers can't hit it or its neighbours. Chariot Ⓒ slides 2 straight with knight squares off each lane. Archer Ⓐ steps 1–2 straight; shoots 12 squares around it (the 5×5 ring without corners or the squares right beside it) without moving. Sapper Ⓢ leaps, never captures; after moving it may place a wall next to itself (once per 2 turns). Catapult Ⓣ walks to any of the 8 squares around it or 1–3 straight; fires on 7 squares ahead (hitting (0,4) also splashes (±1,4)), then reloads for 2 turns (it cannot move at all on the first turn after firing).
  • Jester Ⓙ (replaces the middle Guard; pawns can promote to it) never captures by itself; hops to the opposite-colour squares of its 5×5 and borrows the moves of any non-Imperial piece of its OWN side in its 3×3 (never enemies, never ranged ones, never another Jester). Pawn ♟ 1 step (up to 3 on its first move; 2 in the King's aura), captures diagonally; on the 4-player cross board 2 steps (3 in the aura); the wide 2v2 board is like the 2-player board. The Pikeman still captures only the single square straight ahead. Pikeman ♙ also captures straight. Skirmisher ⓜ also steps sideways. Pikeman → Lancer Ⓛ, Skirmisher → Raider Ⓓ on promotion.

Piece guide

4-player free-for-all (29×29)

  • Cross-shaped board: a 21×21 centre with a 13×4 arm for each army (same 39-piece layout, rotated). Seats take turns in order; last seat standing wins.
  • Promotion happens on the far edge of the centre square (22 steps from your army). Tick Teams for 2v2 (partners sit opposite and never capture or check each other); choose the Wide board for the 30×16 version where each team's two armies sit side by side.
  • Check applies to a seat only when it has exactly one Imperial left, and any opponent can give it. A checkmated seat, or one left with only Imperials, is out; a stalemated seat just skips. Their pieces turn gray: they stop moving but can be captured, Imperials included, with no immunities.

Walls & special rules

  • Walls block movement and lines; they belong to the player whose Sapper raised them (shown in that colour): only enemies can capture one, by moving onto it. Ranged shots pass over them. The number on a wall is how many full rounds it still stands (it vanishes at the start of the enemy's 4th turn in 2-player; lifetime scales with the number of seats).
  • En passant: after a 2–3 step pawn move, an enemy pawn-family piece may capture diagonally onto a square it passed, next turn only.
  • Ranged shots (⌖) capture without moving. Click a piece to see its squares.
